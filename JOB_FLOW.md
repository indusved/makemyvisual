## 1. The job

**Job 1: the owner of a small gifting, fashion or skincare brand, making a Diwali greeting. This is the one I build for this weekend.** (bet, to confirm in the Wed 7 Oct calls)

When my Diwali hampers and orders start going out and I have nothing with my name on it to send my customers or put in the box, I want a greeting with my business name that looks like a real brand made it, so I can thank them on WhatsApp and in every parcel without waiting for a designer. (bet, to confirm in the Wed 7 Oct calls)

Or: when I need Diwali orders now, I want an offer post ("Diwali hampers from ₹999, order by 1 Nov") with my real product. Both go to the Wed 7 Oct calls, and the calls pick one.

Why the two are kept apart: Dhanteras is Fri 6 Nov (31 days away) and Diwali is Sun 8 Nov (33 days) [diwali.info]. Festive shopping starts 3–4 weeks before Diwali, so from about 11 Oct [CleverTap]. A greeting sent in Diwali week arrives after people have chosen where to buy. So the greeting earns goodwill and a card in the gift box; winning Diwali orders this month is the offer ad's job.

Other moments it happens:
- A card to put in every hamper or parcel that goes out (the 4x6 print card).
- Dhanteras, Bhai Dooj, New Year.
- A festive offer, which is the offer ad (for example "Diwali gift hampers from ₹999").
- A new arrival.
- In my words: "Since the gifting season is approaching in India on account of Diwali, the app would likely be used by several small businesses to send greetings and goodies to their network, may not be always through Meta /Google Ads."

Who, by situation (not age, city or title): they run a small gifting, fashion or skincare brand from their phone, sell on Instagram and WhatsApp, have Diwali orders and corporate hampers going out in the next few weeks, and have no designer free to make festive posts. ## 2. The switch

What they'd fire:
- **Job 1:** the hunt through Canva templates, the festival-post app subscription, the freelancer, the generic WhatsApp forward or printed generic tags, or sending nothing.
- **Job 2:** waiting on a designer or an agency (my "3 days to make one ad").

The forces that matter (keep one to four, delete the rest):

Push, outside them (what's going wrong in their life right now): Diwali orders go out in 3–4 weeks and no designer is free in October. (the designer part is a bet, to confirm in the Wed 7 Oct calls)

Pull, inside them (what they've always wanted): look like a real brand without a designer.

Anxiety (the risk of trying you: what they tried before, or a decision that's hard to undo): "It'll look like a cheap forward, or hit me with a watermark or autopay at the end."

Habit (the way they already do it): make it on the phone, send it on WhatsApp, maybe once a year.

What's behind these lines (evidence, not extra forces):
- Push: festive shopping starts 3–4 weeks before Diwali [CleverTap]. D2C beauty brands report festive growth this year: SEREKO skincare 42%, MARS Cosmetics about 60%, and one fragrance brand expects 70–75% [Indian Retailer]. Two festival-post apps got their most 1–2★ reviews in Diwali week (Festival Poster Maker & Post in 2025; DigitalPost in 2023 and 2024) [Play Store reviews]. Separately, at other times, reviews complain about servers going down and blurry posts.
- Anxiety, what these apps have burned them with:
  - A phone number and SMS code (OTP) before they see anything: "just show the app if we like then we can subscribe" [Play Store review, AdBanao, 1★, 2 Oct 2025].
  - A watermark across the post that only appears at download, after the work is done [Play Store reviews, Festival Poster Maker & Post, DigitalPost].
  - A ₹1 or ₹9 trial that turns into a monthly or quarterly automatic payment (UPI autopay): Brands.live's ₹1 trial became ₹499 a quarter; Kaagaz's ₹9 trial became monthly [Play Store reviews].
  - Their name or logo too small, the wrong colour, or missing [Play Store reviews, Brands.live, Festival Poster Maker & Post].
  - The same boring designs everyone else uses [Play Store reviews, AdBanao, Brands.live, Kaagaz].
- Habit: every app above ends on a WhatsApp share. One review says the app isn't for someone who needs it only once or twice [Play Store review, Festival Poster Maker & Post, 1★, 20 Oct 2025].

What the product does about each:
- **Push ->**
  - The link opens the maker straight away (built on my laptop, not live yet; goes live with the ease-of-use pass, due end of Tue 6 Oct).
  - A greeting needs only the business name.
  - Planned: at most 7 taps the first time and 2 the next (not yet counted on a real phone).
  - The date is on screen: "Diwali is on Sunday, 8 November".
- **Pull ->**
  - Their name goes in bold capitals, centred under the greeting, on a finished Diwali design.
  - Each trade gets its own thank-you wording on the greeting; the 4 Diwali backdrops are shared. Offer ads use a backdrop set picked for each trade.
  - It comes in 5 sizes, including a 4x6 card to print for gift boxes.
  - For offer ads, their real product photo is lifted off its background in the browser and placed on a ready-made backdrop. The product is never drawn by AI.
- **Anxiety ->**
  - They see the finished greeting with their own name before giving us anything (built on my laptop, not live yet; goes live with the ease-of-use pass, due end of Tue 6 Oct).
  - We ask for an email and a 6-digit code only when they tap Share or Download (built on my laptop, not live yet; goes live with the ease-of-use pass, due end of Tue 6 Oct).
  - No password, no trial, no autopay, and no phone number required (an optional contact line can hold one). Payment isn't built yet; the plan for Thu 8 Oct is a plain payment link for one job.
  - Greetings carry a small "Made with MakeMyVisual" credit, by my choice (Mon 5 Oct). It shows on the first preview, so it's never a surprise at the end. Offer ads carry no credit. The credit may work against the pull (look like a real brand), so I ask about it in the Wed calls (see section 4, payment).
- **Habit ->**
  - Nothing to install: it opens from a link.
  - Greeting sizes are named by where they go: WhatsApp chat, WhatsApp or Instagram status, Instagram post, Email or LinkedIn, Print card (4x6 in). Offer-ad sizes are named by shape: Square post, Tall post, Status or story, Wide, Small banner.
  - On a phone, the button on WhatsApp-size greetings (chat and status) reads "Share on WhatsApp"; other sizes say "Share", and computers say "Download this picture". Share opens the phone's own sharing menu (the share sheet).
  - Plan: a one-time price for the set, no subscription (not built; price not set, see section 6).

The one worry onboarding must remove: "Will it look like a real brand sent it, or like the same forward everyone gets with my name stamped small in a corner?" (bet, to confirm in the Wed 7 Oct calls)
- How the product answers it: the first thing they see is their own name in bold capitals, centred under the greeting with a gold line above it (not in a corner), on a finished Diwali design with a thank-you line written for their trade.
- Not checked yet: the "Happy Diwali" line can be drawn up to about four times taller than the name. Ask in the calls whether the name reads big enough.
- "No sign-up before you see it" stays as what onboarding step 1 removes.

## 3. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before your product):
1. Their Diwali hampers and orders are about to go out, and they have nothing with their name on it to send customers or put in the box.
2. They open Canva or a festival-post app, or message a freelancer.
3. A festival-post app asks for a phone number and an SMS code, then business name, logo, address and a category, before any post. Brands.live's flow is 6 steps, and its own guide says under 6 minutes from sign-up to first download [Brands.live, App Store]. (Canva lets you edit first and asks you to sign in when you save or share.)
4. They pick from Diwali templates that every other shop uses too. Canva shows about 4,289 Diwali Instagram templates (from a search summary; I couldn't open the page).
5. They fix their name and logo, which often come out small, the wrong colour, or missing [Play Store reviews].
6. They tap download and hit a watermark, or a ₹1–₹9 trial that turns into a monthly or quarterly automatic payment [Play Store reviews].
7. They redo it for status, Instagram and email, and send a file to a printer for the gift-box cards: about ₹5 a card, 3–5 working days [Tagsen].
8. They share it on WhatsApp.

Where it ends today: a post that looks like everyone else's, a watermarked one, a freelancer's post days later, or nothing at all. For me at the agency: "Last week, took me 3 days to make one ad".

With my product (built on my laptop, not live yet; goes live with the ease-of-use pass, due end of Tue 6 Oct):
1. Their Diwali hampers and orders are about to go out, and they want something with their name on it for customers and for the box.
2. They tap makemyvisual.com/gifting (or /fashion, /skincare) in a WhatsApp message or an Instagram bio.
3. The product opens the maker straight away, with "Diwali greeting" already picked and no sign-up screen.
4. They tap the name box ("Your business's name" on /gifting, "Your brand's name" on /fashion and /skincare) and type it (tap 1). A photo and a contact line are optional.
5. They tap "Make my greeting", which stays fixed at the bottom of the screen (tap 2).
6. The product shows their greeting at the top: their name in bold under the greeting on a festive backdrop, WhatsApp size first, 5 sizes to flip through, with the small credit already visible.
7. They tap "Share on WhatsApp" (tap 3).
8. The product asks for their email right under the preview. They tap the box, type it, and tap "Send me a code" (taps 4–5).
9. They read the 6-digit code from the email notification, tap the code box and type it. It sends itself once all 6 digits are in (tap 6).
10. The product saves their business and the greeting, and says "Saved. Tap Share on WhatsApp to send it."
11. They tap "Share on WhatsApp" (tap 7), and the share sheet opens with their greeting.
12. They post it to their WhatsApp status and the chats they pick, or save it. The greeting stays saved in their account, ready for Diwali week; coming back to send it takes 2 taps (planned).

Timing, said plainly: in the sprint window, success is that they make it, sign in, and share or download at least one size. The real Diwali send happens about 2–8 Nov, after submission (Sat 17 Oct). Navratri runs Sun 11 to Mon 19 Oct, so it starts inside the sprint; Dussehra is Tue 20 Oct, 3 days after the sprint ends [Samvat]. That timing favours the offer ad for anything that has to go out this month.

Things it takes to get the job done today: 4+ screens before they see their own post (festival app), 4 sizes rebuilt by hand, plus a printer order · With my product: 1 screen before the preview and 0 sizes rebuilt (planned).
- Screens before they see their own post: festival app 4+ (phone and code, business details, category, template); Canva 0 sign-in screens, but a hunt through thousands of templates; mine 1. The live site today still shows 4 screens, 3 typed fields and a trip to the email app first.
- Sizes they rebuild by hand after the first: today 4 (status, Instagram, email, print card) plus a printer order; mine 0.
- Taps to a shareable greeting with mine: planned at most 7 the first time and 2 the next, plus typing the name, email and code.
- Waiting: today, a designer (3 days for my last ad) or 3–5 working days for printed cards; mine, no designer.
- My counts are the plan's limits for the flow as built. They haven't been checked on a real phone or with a real user.

What must not happen:
- **Step 2–3:** a sign-up wall, a required phone number, or another vertical's page (a gift shop seeing cars).
- **Step 4 and 6:** their name misspelled, cut off or too small. This matters most on the 4x6 print card, which can't be fixed once it's printed. The product puts exactly what they type on the card, so the preview has to make the name easy to check.
- **Step 6:** a long blank wait. Or, when they add a photo, the product looking fake, floating or changed ("the car seems to be floating in the air!", "Car doesn't blend in"). The product is never drawn by AI.
- **Step 8–9:** losing their work while they switch to the email app for the code, or the code email landing in spam or never arriving (sign-in codes not arriving is a known complaint about Kaagaz, by SMS [Play Store review]). Built so far, not live:
  - After a page reload, their words, photo and the open code step come back.
  - The code step names the email's subject line and says to check Spam and Promotions.
  - "Send a new code" appears after 30 seconds.
- **Step 11:** the share sheet not opening after sign-in. Phones only open it straight after a tap, which is why there's a second Share tap. Not tested on a real phone yet.
- **Step 12:** promising "to all your customers". Sending to a big customer list is a WhatsApp broadcast list, which we don't control, and WhatsApp caps how many chats one share can reach (current limit not checked).
- **Any step:** a watermark or a payment request that only shows up at the end.

Other flows (stop when the job is covered):
- Fashion greeting with a photo of an outfit (the outfit is shown hanging): 10 taps.
- Offer ad for gifting, fashion or skincare from a gallery photo: 11 taps.
- Car offer ad from makemyvisual.com/cars. A photo is needed; a quick version shows within about 2 seconds, then the showroom version swaps in by itself: 11 taps.
- Car Diwali greeting: 8 taps.
- Coming back signed in: a greeting in 2 taps, a car offer ad in 6.
- All counts and times are planned limits, not measured.

That covers the job.

## 4. Onboarding

First value (the moment it first does the job for them): their own business name on a finished Diwali greeting, WhatsApp size, at the top of the screen right after they tap "Make my greeting", before any sign-in. (built on my laptop, not live yet; goes live with the ease-of-use pass, due end of Tue 6 Oct)

The smallest commitment we ask for: their business name, which is one field. Nothing else until they choose to share or download. Then it's an email and a 6-digit code: no password, no payment, no phone number required.

The worry it removes (from section 2): "Will it look like a real brand sent it, or like the same forward everyone gets with my name stamped small in a corner?" (bet, to confirm in the Wed 7 Oct calls)

From opening the link to the first value:
1. They tap makemyvisual.com/gifting in a WhatsApp message or an Instagram bio, and the maker opens straight away with the Diwali greeting already picked. Removes: "Do I have to install an app and give my number just to look?"
2. They type their business name, the only required field, and tap "Make my greeting". Removes: "Will I have to learn a design tool or dig through thousands of templates?"
3. Their greeting appears: their name in bold capitals under the greeting, on a finished Diwali design with a thank-you line for their trade, WhatsApp size first, with Status, Instagram, Email and the 4x6 gift-box card one tap away. That's the first value, and it answers the worry if the name reads big enough (to check in the calls).

Login: after step 3, on their first tap of Share or Download.
- Only then is there something worth keeping.
- The email lets us save it, bring it back next time, and count a real signup (an email plus a first visual, which is how the Revenue track counts signups).
- Sign-in is an email code from hello@makemyvisual.com.
- Status: built on my laptop, not live yet. The live version (since Mon 5 Oct) still asks people to sign in first: 4 screens, 3 typed fields and a trip to the email app before anything shows.

What we don't ask on day one:
- Phone number required: no. An optional contact line (phone, website or Instagram) sits in the form before the preview; it goes on the visual and is kept after the first save.
- Password, logo, address.
- A category from a long list (the link already sets the vertical).
- India or US: the app guesses from the phone's time zone, and it can be changed in Edit business.
- A product photo (optional for greetings).
- Camera or gallery permission, until they tap a photo button.
- A tour, notifications, a payment or a trial.

What we ask later, and when:
- Email and a 6-digit code: after their first tap of Share or Download.
- A product photo: after they switch to "Offer ad" or tap "+ Add a photo".
- Payment: proposal, not decided (my call before Thu 8 Oct; not built). Shown on the first preview as the price of the full set: "Free: WhatsApp size with a small MakeMyVisual credit. ₹199: all 5 sizes including the print card, no credit." The free WhatsApp size stays free. Why: a Diwali greeting is a once-a-year job, so if the first full set is free and the price only comes after they send it, nothing is left to buy. This would change my Mon 5 Oct choice to keep the credit on every greeting, so I ask about the credit in the Wed calls first.

## 6. The riskiest guess

If this is false, the product is pointless: an owner who sees a greeting with their own name on it will pay ₹199 before Diwali for the full set (5 sizes including the 4x6 gift-box card, without our credit) instead of making one free in Canva. (bet; ₹199 is a proposed price, my call before sending)
- Evidence for: a design studio's own guide prices social posts at ₹300–1,500 each [Kalex], and one Diwali poster job got 61 bids averaging ₹1,321 [Freelancer.com], so people do pay for one-off festive posts.
- Not evidence for a one-off greeting: Brands.live says 150,000+ people pay (its own claim), but for daily festival posts all year [Brands.live].
- Evidence against: Canva Pro is free with some Jio plans from 16 Sep 2026 [Mobigyaan]. Crafto (10Cr+ installs) puts your name on free greetings every day [Play Store]. One festival-app review says it isn't for someone who needs it only once or twice [Play Store review, Festival Poster Maker & Post, 1★, 20 Oct 2025]. No business owner has said they'd pay yet (0 conversations). Zero real users have tried MakeMyVisual so far.

Thirty-minute check, no code (run it while you build), and what happened:
1. Pick 3 gifting or fashion businesses I can reach on WhatsApp who aren't friends.
2. Make each one's greeting with their real business name. The live app or Canva by hand both work, but the no-credit full set has to be made by hand for now: the app always adds the credit to greetings.
3. Send the WhatsApp size with: "Made this for your Diwali list. The full set (WhatsApp, status, Instagram, email and a 4x6 card for your hampers, without our name on it) is ₹199. Pay to [my UPI ID] and I'll send all 5 in 10 minutes."
